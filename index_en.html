Hello, I am Chun-Hao Wen, an undergraduate student majoring in Mathematics at National Taiwan Normal University (2022-2026). During my first five semesters, I immersed myself in the rigorous training of pure mathematics, especially Algebra, delving deeply into Linear Algebra, Abstract Algebra, Galois Theory, and Homological Algebra. However, after careful consideration, I decided to leverage this solid theoretical foundation to formally step into the fields of applied mathematics and data science.

Starting with the applications of the Graph Laplacian, I applied my familiar algebraic perspective to delve into the spectral theory of functional analysis. Beyond analyzing its geometric properties in Spectral Clustering, I further explored advanced Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN). Relying on the rigorous logic cultivated through pure mathematics, I progressively unlocked the doors to the machine learning domain.

From implementing a brain tumor MRI segmentation system and developing quantitative trading algorithms based on graph theory and machine learning, to applying advanced algorithms to analyze real-world medical data on myocardial infarction mechanisms, I continuously expand my cross-disciplinary engineering and statistical skills. I aspire to keep translating abstract mathematical logic into practical perspectives for solving real-world problems, ultimately carving out my own niche in the fields of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence.

Algebra explores the "structural" nature of mathematics. When writing code or analyzing real-world problems, algebra and linear algebra help me rapidly construct clear mathematical models in my mind. Linear relationships are ubiquitous in real life; especially in computer science, the concepts of matrices and vector spaces serve as the core foundational logic for data processing, graph architectures, and modern machine learning.

Probability and statistics are the sciences of quantifying "uncertainty," providing a rigorous mathematical perspective for ML. They enable me to uncover the underlying distribution patterns of data and accurately assess risks. When facing real-world data full of noise or complex structures, this knowledge allows me to build robust prediction models tailored to various scenarios and establish objective, solid criteria for evaluating model performance by strictly clarifying different types of errors.

Scientific computing is the critical bridge connecting pure mathematical theory with computer implementation. While pursuing theoretical precision, handling inevitable computational errors and limit approximations in practice is crucial. It trained me to build a mindset centered on "computational cost," "algorithm efficiency," and "convergence," applying rigorous mathematical knowledge to provide precise ideas and strict proofs for efficient code that solves real-world problems.

Currently studying and actively researching in this domain.

Functional analysis is a core mathematical branch exploring function spaces and operator theory. To thoroughly understand the foundational logic of Spectral Theory within Graph Laplacian, I initiated a self-study of this subject. By analyzing the properties of infinite-dimensional spaces and operator behaviors, I aim to gain a macroscopic perspective on optimization problems in machine learning and lay a solid theoretical foundation for delving into model generalization.

Graph Laplacian & Spectral Clustering: Applications in Geometric Models, Urban Networks, and Medical Imaging

Utilized the Stanford Bunny geometric model to demonstrate the core of the algorithm, and empirically validated sociology theories (like urban economics) that are typically hard to prove by integrating modern ML methods. Later applied this algorithmic framework to the medical AI field, accurately identifying brain tumor images to assist clinical diagnoses, aiming to effectively reduce the workload of medical professionals.

The second phase of the research introduced Feature Extraction Engineering to segment MRI images into tumor and non-tumor regions. Future work aims to incorporate the Dice Coefficient for continuous improvement, developing a clinical assistant tool.

Note

This project was conducted jointly with Hsin-Yi Ko, a fellow student in the Department of Mathematics. My primary contributions included mathematical and theoretical study, research conception and methodology design, algorithm implementation, and subsequent extensions; Hsin-Yi Ko primarily contributed through joint discussions and oral presentations.

As the project continued to evolve, successive stages were presented at the 2025 Intercollegiate Applied Mathematics Conference, the 2026 TWSIAM Annual Meeting, and ICOML 2026.

Factor GCN for End-to-End Portfolio Optimization

In collaboration with KGI Securities × KGI Securities Investment Advisory

View Project ↗ View Poster ↗

To overcome the pain points of traditional portfolios being susceptible to "spurious correlations" and the HRP model being limited by historical price noise, this research proposed a Factor GCN model based on graph theory architecture. Through Graph Convolutional Networks, the algorithm deeply fuses long-term fundamental factors with short-term momentum features, effectively filtering market noise, and dynamically outputs optimal asset allocation weights in an end-to-end manner to maximize the return per unit of risk.

Note

This is an independent research project advised by 戴立庭, a quantitative researcher at KGI Securities, who provided financial-domain knowledge and guidance on research direction and model refinement.

The project was presented at the 2026 Scientific Computing Student Seminar_Quantitative Trading and ICOML 2026.
